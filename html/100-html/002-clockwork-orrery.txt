Orbits must be TRUE ellipses, not circles: elements (a, e, T, longitude of perihelion, mean longitude at
J2000, inclination) for Mercury…Uranus are hard-coded (Neptune is deliberately omitted by the maker and
the copy says why). Each frame, for the simulated Julian day jd = 2412464.5 + days (epoch 1893 Jan 1.0),
compute mean longitude, mean anomaly M, then solve Kepler's M = E − e sin E by Newton's iteration
(seed E = M for e < 0.8 else π; tolerance 1e-12 rad; at most 12 visits), then true anomaly
ν = 2·atan2(√(1+e)·sin(E/2), √(1−e)·cos(E/2)) and radius r = a(1 − e cos E). Rings are drawn from the
SAME E-parametrisation as the planets (x = a cos E − a e, y = a√(1−e²) sin E, squashed by K), so a planet
can never drift off its own engraved ellipse and the Sun sits exactly at the focus. Each planet is a
radial-gradient sphere carrying a per-planet linear-gradient "night" overlay whose gradientTransform is
set every frame to rotate(atan2 of the anti-solar direction), giving a terminator that genuinely points
away from the Sun; Saturn adds a back ring ellipse plus a front arc; Earth carries a dashed lunar circle
and a Moon on a 27.321661 d orbit with its own terminator. Behind every body draw a brightened trail
path spanning an equal interval of time (clamp(0.028·T, 5, 34) days) so its length evidences the second
law. The Sun is a corona radial gradient, sixteen engraved rays and a #gSun disc labelled CENTRUM SOLIS.

In the lower-left corner of the case, a glazed "reveal window" shows a real gear train: three wheels
generated in JS as toothed paths on a common module MOD = 2.3 (addendum 0.95 m, dedendum 1.2 m; root
arc → straight flank → tip arc → flank per tooth) with 54, 24 and 36 teeth, centre distances
(r1 + r2) so they mesh, and angular velocities ω = 1, −2.25, +1.5 (inverse tooth counts, alternating
sign). Wheels get lightening holes, hubs, arbors and shadow discs. A second window (hidden below
1000 px) shows a lever escapement: a 15-tooth escape wheel that ratchets 12° per beat with a cubic-eased
engage, a dead-beat anchor with patina pallet stones rocking at 0.82× the pendulum's ±5.2°, a crutch and
bob on a 2 s period, a 1 Hz beat lamp and a FREE/HELD word. Below the dial a brass control rail holds
rate buttons 1×/60×/360×/1800× (aria-pressed), Hold/Resume and Epoch buttons, and mono readouts for the
dial date (Julian Day → Gregorian by the 2299161 algorithm), the Julian day to 3 dp and days from epoch.
Under the instrument: an engraved schedule table of the elements (a, e, T, ϖ, i, dial radius in inches,
driver/planet wheel teeth) built from the same data; a two-column method section stating Kepler's
equation and the Newton update with a live iteration panel printing the seed, every E_n to 12 dp, the
correction, the visit count and the resulting ν; a three-column provenance essay (Graham and Rowley,
the Earl of Orrery, Benjamin Martin's universal orrery, Herschel's Georgium Sidus); and a footer.

ANIMATION: requestAnimationFrame; days += rate·dt with rate in days of ephemeris per real second

DESIGN: An exhibition plate rather than a dashboard: the whole page is warm black lacquer and hand-divided brass, and every affordance is a piece of the instrument (a reveal window, a rivetted maker's plate, a control rail of milled buttons). The only brightness is metal — engraved rings, screw slots, a gilt Sun — so the black ground reads as ebony and the page feels lit by one lamp in a museum case. The typography does the period work (Didot caps engraved with light-on-dark text-shadows, mono for anything numerical, Palatino text at 1.66 leading), and the copy stays in character throughout, admitting honestly that the orbit radii are reduced for the dial while the anomalies are left true.

TECHNIQUES: one <style> and one <script>, no external references, no images (grain = inline feTurbulence; vignette = radial-gradient; all artwork = SVG/Canvas); SVG geometry generated in JS via createElementNS; Kepler's equation solved by Newton iteration each frame; ellipse rings and planet positions sharing one eccentric-anomaly parametrisation; per-planet linear-gradient terminators rotated by gradientTransform about the objectBoundingBox centre; equal-time trail arcs as evidence of Kepler's second law; JS-generated involute-ish gear tooth paths on a common module with true ω ∝ 1/N ratios and meshing centre distances; ratcheted escape wheel (floor + cubic-eased fraction) for a plausible dead-beat escapement; Julian-Day↔Gregorian conversion for a live 1893 date; live Newton-iteration printout; tabular-nums mono readouts; clamp()-based fluid typography and spacing; inline gradient-filled case furniture (corner brackets, slotted screws, maker's plate) instead of images.
