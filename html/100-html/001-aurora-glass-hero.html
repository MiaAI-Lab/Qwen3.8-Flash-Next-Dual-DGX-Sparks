    How we score the night

    A raw magnetometer trace is mostly noise: trams, transformers, the tide flexing the bedrock under the sensor. We subtract the quiet-day curve for each station, square the residual in the 10–150 mHz window, and integrate over the auroral hours. The result is a per-station disturbance that we map back to a magnetic latitude and then to the familiar nine-step index.

    The forecast half of the problem is simpler than it looks and much harder to get right. Sixty-two minutes after the L1 monitor tastes a stream, we know roughly what the oval will do — provided the interplanetary magnetic field holds its southward component. That single number, Bz, is why our confidence envelope is a wedge and not a line.

    — H. Lindqvist, duty forecaster, night 47
