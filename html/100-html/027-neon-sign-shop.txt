TITLE: Vessel & Filament — Neon Sign Works, Closing Time (Bench 3)

PROMPT:
Build a single self-contained HTML page: a neon sign workshop at closing time, last customer
gone, the sign still burning. Everything drawn in-browser — no CDN, no web fonts, no images,
no libraries, system font stacks only, one <style> block and one <script> block, no console
errors, responsive, prefers-reduced-motion honoured.
- A wet-brick storefront at night. The brick must come from layered gradients alone (offset
  courses with broken joints, a lit top lip and shadowed bottom per brick, grime wash, damp
  blotches), with a shop window of unlit bent stock, a doorway, a service conduit and a
  transformer on the wall. Below the facade, a wet street that MIRRORS the sign and wobbles.
- NEON SIGN EDITOR: an editable line of text (contenteditable) rendered as glowing glass tubes
  using layered text-shadows — white-hot core, coloured halo, wide bloom. A second, smaller
  tube line below it, plus a bent tube swash drawn as an inline SVG path under the words.
  Whatever is typed must appear in the puddle at the same time.
- GAS-COLOUR SWATCHES recolour the whole run instantly: Argon #35c8ff, Neon coral #ff4d6d,
  Krypton #b6ff3a, Xenon amber #ffb42e, Mercury violet #a06bff. The wall glow, the window
  spill and the reflection all take the new colour.
- CONTROLS: FLICKER — one tube stutters with irregular, non-uniform timing (never a plain CSS
  loop); BURN RATE — a brightness slider that everything reads; a tube-character selector that
  switches the lettering between script (cursive stack, tight tracking), geometric caps and mono.
- AMBIENT LIFE: a buzzing transformer (real WebAudio buzz, click to audition), a moth orbiting
  the sign, occasional rain that dims and refracts the glow, and an OPEN/SOLD-OUT flip sign
  that toggles the shop between full burn and a night burn.
Make it look like a photograph of a real shop at 23:47, not like a UI demo.

DESIGN:
Art direction is "one light source in a dead city": everything on the page is either the tube
or something the tube is touching, so the palette is a near-black masonry base (night #050409,
brick #0b0908/#170f0c/#2a1a14, mortar #120c09, asphalt #08080c, kerb #131318) against a single
saturated gas colour that the user chooses. Because the accent is a variable rather than a
fixed hue, the page can be coral or acid green or violet without touching a rule — the gas
drives --gas/--gas-soft/--gas-far, and every glow, the wall wash, the window spill, the slider
fill, the selection colour and the moody blue bench lamp all follow it. Typography splits by
role: a mono stack (ui-monospace/SFMono/Menlo/Consolas) for everything mechanical — rail
markings, plate labels, meters, buttons, the door faces — set wide (0.14–0.34em) like
screen-printed steel; a humanist sans (Avenir Next/Helvetica Neue/Futura) for the bench
prose; and three stacks reserved for the tubes themselves (Snell Roundhand/Apple Chancery
cursive with negative tracking for bent script glass, Futura caps at 0.17em for geometric
runs, heavy mono for industrial lettering). Composition is a two-column workbench: the
storefront on the left, a sticky steel control panel on the right, a shop clock and a status
line as the frame — the reader is a sign-maker standing at Bench 3 at close, not a visitor
to a website. Deliberate imperfections carry the fiction: one tube is always weak, the bricks
are stained and dripped, the reflection is blurred and never still, the meter reports a glass
temperature, and the copy talks about electrodes and strikes rather than features.

TECHNIQUES:
- Brick from stacked CSS gradients only: two repeating-linear-gradient courses plus a
  .wall::before half-course offset by half a brick and cut with a repeating mask-image so the
  joints break; .wall::after adds a lit lip and a shadowed underside with mix-blend-mode:screen.
- Neon lettering as seven stacked text-shadows on one element (1px white core, 4px white halo,
  9/18px gas, 34px gas-soft, 68px + 120px gas-far bloom) plus a filter:
  brightness(calc(.55 + .55*--burn - .3*--damp)) saturate(...) on the same node.
- Two custom properties as a global lighting bus: --burn (0.45–1.25) and --damp (0–1) written
  to :root by JS; opacity, filter and colour stops everywhere use calc() against them, so one
  number relights the wall, the puddle, the swash and the mirror.
- Per-glyph typesetting: each character of the editable lines is wrapped in <span class="ch">
  with caret position captured/restored by character index through a Range, so individual
  tubes can go dead (.dead), stutter (.jitter, steps(1)) or be mirrored one letter at a time.
- Inline SVG: the bent tube swash (two paths, a wide gas stroke plus a thin white fill stroke,
  stroke-linecap round, drop-shadow() filter chain, stroke-dasharray/dashoffset gas-flow pulse)
  with electrode rects; the unlit tube rack in the shop window; the transformer arc spark; the
  moth (two <g> wings animated by a scaleX flap keyframe, body ellipse, antennae strokes).
- 3D door sign: perspective on the button, transform-style:preserve-3d on the leaf, two faces
  with backface-visibility and rotateX(±180deg), aria-pressed also cross-fades face opacity so
  the visible face is deterministic; the leaf is forced to display:block because it is a span.
- Wet street mirror: a duplicated copy of both tube lines and the swash inside a container
  masked with a linear-gradient mask-image, flipped with translateY(100%) scaleY(-1) (origin is
  the top edge, so a bare scaleY(-1) throws the copy out of the clipped street), blurred and
  saturated, then wobbled every frame by JS summing two sines into scaleY/skewX/translateY.
  .ripples uses repeating gradients with mix-blend-mode:overlay and an animated
  background-position.
- Canvas rain: fixed full-viewport <canvas>, drops as short slanted strokes whose count
  (50 + 210*damp), alpha and speed scale with the damp variable, stroke colour mixed from the
  current gas RGB, device-pixel-ratio-aware backing store capped at 2x.
- WebAudio transformer: four oscillators (100Hz triangle plus 150/200.6/300.4Hz sawtooth) into
  a 340Hz lowpass with Q 5, into a gain ramped to 0.026, with a 47Hz square LFO summed into the
  gain param for the buzz; nodes are ramped down and stopped on toggle-off. A short per-gas
  sine blip (262–494Hz, exponential decay) sounds only while the hum is already running.
- Irregular animation from JS, not keyframes: the flicker engine picks a weighted random branch
  (long dead hold 900–2800ms / burst of 2–5 half-ignitions with 38–210ms gaps / single 50–240ms
  miss then 800–4600ms of calm) and sags the --burn variable with it, so the wall glow and the
  reflection stutter along with the letter. A strike() walks a five-step dip plan; sag() and
  throatClear() fire on a recursive 19–51s ambient scheduler.
- Forced-reflow class restart (remove class, read offsetWidth, re-add) to replay the stutter
  keyframe on one glyph; requestAnimationFrame drives a single loop for wobble, moth, rain and
  the eased damp value, cancelled on visibilitychange.
- Screen-blend radial gradients (.wall-tint, .spill) so the gas colour adds light instead of
  painting over the brick; CSS grid with minmax(0,1fr) columns and collapsing areas at
  1080/640/400px; segmented buttons and range inputs styled from scratch (webkit and moz
  pseudo-elements, track fill from a JS-updated --fill variable); prefers-reduced-motion both
  as a CSS block and as a matchMedia gate that removes the rAF loop entirely.

INTERACTION:
Two contenteditable tube lines on the sign board: #lineMain (default "Neon Alley", hard cap 22
characters) and #lineSub (default "bend & fill · after midnight", cap 34). Typing re-typesets
both the line and its mirror in the street, restores the caret to the same character index, and
re-picks the weak tube if the text got shorter; Enter blurs instead of inserting a break;
pasting is flattened to single spaces; emptying a line on blur restores the stock copy and says
so. #lineSub and the mouse are also plain text — nothing else on the page is editable.
Fill gas: five .gas buttons (also keyboard G, which cycles). Clicking sets --gas/--gas-soft/
--gas-far from that button's hex, moves aria-pressed, updates the "Fill gas" readout, the
Fill meter and the status line, re-tints the rain and the slider fill, and plays a short
pitched blip only if the transformer is already humming.
Burn rate: #burn range, 45–125, updates --burn, the burnVal percentage, the track fill and the
Draw/Glass meters; crossing the extremes produces a warning line. Keyboard B steps the presets
0.55 / 0.80 / 1.00 / 1.20.
Tube character: three .seg buttons swap font-script / font-caps / font-mono on <body>, which
changes both tube lines' font stack, tracking and (for caps) capitalisation; the active one
keeps aria-pressed and the label reads back script/caps/mono.
Flicker (button or F): picks one non-space glyph as the failing tube and drives it with the
randomised timing engine described above, dimming that glyph (.dead), replaying a 90ms steps(1)
stutter (.jitter) and sagging the global burn. Off restores it and reports a new electrode.
Rain (button or R): turns the canvas shower on, eases --damp to 0.42–0.82 (labels dry / mist /
steady rain / downpour), dims the tube filter, blurs the wall tint and the mirror, and starts
bead streaks running down the board; a user switch locks auto-weather off until the next
shower window (auto showers fire roughly every 28–64s otherwise).
Hum (button or the transformer itself, H): builds or tears down the WebAudio buzz on the click,
flashes the transformer's arc SVG and reports it. Strike (button or S): re-fires the run
through a five-beat dip sequence, arcs the transformer, increments the Strikes counter.
Door sign (#flip, or O): flips the leaf between OPEN and SOLD OUT via aria-pressed, toggles
.sold-out on <body>, rewrites the footer door state, and multiplies the whole lighting bus by
0.44 for a night burn — flipping back to OPEN runs a strike so the sign visibly re-fires.
Ambient, no input needed: the shop clock ticks from 23:47:06, the moth orbits the measured
centre of the board and fades in, the mains sag now and then, the status line under the footer
narrates every action, and the puddle mirror never stops moving.
Reduced motion: no rAF loop, no moth, no falling rain (the street is simply wet), the flicker
switch holds the weak tube dark instead of stuttering, and the mirror is a static flip.
Escape blurs an editable tube; shortcuts are ignored while focus is in a tube or a form control.
