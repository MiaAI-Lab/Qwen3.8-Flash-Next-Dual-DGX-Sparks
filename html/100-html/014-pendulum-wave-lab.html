Pendulum
Wave Laboratory

Twenty-four bobs hang from one machined bar, each a little shorter than the last. Their periods are tuned so that the slowest completes exactly 15 swings while the fastest completes 38 — which means the row shears into a snake, a checkerboard, a braid, and then snaps back into a single straight line. Everything below is integrated properly: θ̈ = −(g/L)·sin θ − c·θ̇, fourth-order Runge–Kutta, fixed 1/480 s step.

Operating
the bench

Release the row and watch the near field first: within four seconds the fast bobs on the right have gained a quarter of a swing on the slow bobs on the left, and the straight line has become a lazy wave. It tightens into a braid, dissolves into what looks like noise, and then — with no warning at all — every bob is back on the release line at once. Half a beat earlier they were perfectly mirrored, which is the same pattern with the sign reversed.

The machine has one moving idea: identical phase differences accumulate into a spatial shape. Nothing is coupled. No bob knows about any other bob. The wave is purely a picture your eye makes out of 24 independent clocks.

Why the
taper is quadratic

A pendulum's period goes as √L, so to make the periods step in equal increments — the only way a wave can march down a row — the lengths must go as the square of a harmonic series. Bob n is set to make exactly 14+n complete swings per beat, which forces Ln ∝ 1/(14+n)². The result is a curve, not a staircase: the long end of the row is nearly flat, and the short end collapses hard into the bar.

What the taper cannot survive is a large release angle. The true period grows with amplitude, so every bob runs a little slow, and because the slow bobs complete fewer swings per beat, the phase error accumulates unevenly along the row: released at 10° the fan closes to within about 1° of itself, at 16° it lands roughly 8° wide, and at 34° it never really closes at all. Damping behaves the opposite way to intuition — it shrinks every amplitude together, so the pattern stays razor-sharp while the whole machine quietly deflates.
