TITLE:  Ferroflux — Normal-Field Instability Cabinet (exhibit RF-05)

PROMPT:  Author one premium self-contained page about a ferrofluid demonstration
cabinet: the Rosensweig normal-field instability, spike formation, coil geometry and
colloid composition. Art direction: a pale, precise laboratory ground — warm graph
paper, NOT a dark neon scene — where one black mirror pool is the only dark mass and
hairline field lines appear in a single accent; the register is a scientific exhibit
panel, not sci-fi. Palette: #f1eee7 #e3ded2 #1a1815 #0d0c0b (the fluid) plus one accent
(#4a5fc7) and a neutral #8b8578. Type: display Didot, "Bodoni MT", "Hoefler Text",
Optima, serif; body "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, system-ui; readouts ui-monospace, Menlo.
Layout: a fluid canvas stage as the hero with a right-hand instrument column (field mT,
coil current, gap, composition) and three lower diagram plates — surfactant-coated
nanoparticle colloid, the force balance that sets spike spacing, a coil cross-section —
plus an exhibition caption carrying real numbers. Motion: the field ramp must genuinely
transition flat mirror -> ridges -> rising spikes with a travelling specular highlight,
using your own metaball/marching-squares field plus value noise; reduced-motion renders
the settled state only. Interaction: a field slider 0-400 mT driving a computed spike
wavelength (surface tension vs magnetic stress vs gravity) that refuses spikes below
onset, a coil geometry selector, a gap slider, a field-pulse button, click-a-spike to
read height and local field, and hover on the composition plate to reveal layer labels.

DESIGN:  The page is drawn like a museum bench card that happens to be running an
experiment. Everything sits on warm graph paper (#f1eee7 with a 9 px minor and 45 px
major ruled grid painted by four repeating-linear-gradients), so the only saturated dark
mass on screen is the pool itself (#0d0c0b with a #211e1b sheen at its surface); the
pole piece, the coil windings and the colloid bodies in the plates are engineering
section-hatching in #e3ded2 and #8b8578 rather than solid blacks, which keeps the dark
pool unique and reads as laboratory drawing. #4a5fc7 is the single accent and is used
only for field: field lines, dimension rules, the selected-wavenumber marker, the live
field value. Didot/Bodoni carries the masthead, deck and plate titles at high contrast
against Helvetica body text and monospace instrument values, so the hierarchy is
"wall label / paragraph / dial". The stage is a bordered 16:9 tank with corner register
marks, a dashed rest-surface line, a 10 mm scale bar and a λ dimension rule; the
instrument column is a dial strip with tabular numerals and a 12 s B(t) trace whose
dashed onset line the trace visibly crosses. Horizontal scale in the tank is true 1:1
across the 96 mm specimen; only the vertical gap is exaggerated, by a flat 1.35x printed
in the plate's own note ("gap drawn x1.35"), so the dimension rule, the ceiling and the
pole plate all move together and the drawing never claims a scale it is not using.

TECHNIQUES:  Canvas 2D stage with DPR scaling. Free-surface profile: a phase
accumulator integrates 1/lambda(x) so a radially non-uniform coil field bends the crest
array; crest shape blends a cosine ridge into a rounded cone ((1-|s|)^2 + r^2)^0.5 - r
with the mix driven by overdrive, so the ramp genuinely passes mirror -> ridge -> spike;
three-octave value noise (hashed lattice, cosine interpolation) jitters amplitude, adds
the sub-onset capillary dimple field and drives the wall envelope. The surface is then
re-embedded as a metaball scalar field (flat term y - profile plus one compact
poly6-style ball per tip) sampled on a 6 px grid; a hand-written 16-case marching-squares
pass with bilinear edge interpolation emits the isocline segments, which are stroked as
a rim-lit hairline inside the clipped fluid path, while the fill polygon comes from the
topmost phi = 0 crossing per css column (two smoothing passes). Specular: per-column
surface normals from the silhouette are compared with a slowly rotating 2-D light
direction, pow(n.l, 26), summed with a gaussian band whose centre travels as
0.5 + 0.62*sin(0.30 t), drawn as short pale strokes on top of the black — plus radial
gradient glints at each crest. Field lines are streamlines integrated 460 steps at a time
through a superposition of the uniform normal field and one attractor per crest, so flux
genuinely concentrates into the spikes. Physics: Langevin M(H) from the real 9.4 nm
particle moment; driving stress S = COUPLE * mu0 * INT M dH (cumulative Simpson over a
260-point field grid, one constant fitted to the measured 17.5 mT onset); dispersion
rho*omega^2 = sigma*k^3 + rho*g*k - S*k^2; k_c = sqrt(rho*g/sigma) and S_c =
2*sqrt(rho*g*sigma) give lambda_c = 8.60 mm at onset and lambda = lambda_c *
exp(-0.155*sqrt(Bo_m - 1)) above it; crest height is room*tanh(0.22*overdrive + 0.05) with
room = min(0.42 x gap, 0.55 x lambda_c), so spikes saturate instead of piercing the pole
piece; tip field from crest sharpness; coil current from the Helmholtz axis formula with a
per-geometry factor. Plate I and III are built with createElementNS (radial surfactant
tails, head groups, carrier molecules, dipole arrows, scale bar; clipped diagonal
hatching, winding cross-sections with turn circles, conical pole polygons, computed gap
field paths). Plate II redraws the omega^2/k curve, its unstable lobe and the selected k
from the live field every ~420 ms. SVG trace polyline for B(t).

INTERACTION:  Field slider 0-400 mT sets the target field; the displayed field
exponentially approaches it (rate 4.6/s) so the surface moves through mirror, ridges and
spikes rather than switching. Gap slider 3-24 mm moves the hatched pole plate, its
dimension rule and the crest ceiling. Three coil-geometry buttons (Helmholtz k=1.00,
bore solenoid k=0.86, conical poles k=1.35) switch aria-pressed state, change the radial
field gradient used by the profile and the streamline/plate drawings, and rebuild Plate
III and its caption. "Field pulse" runs a 2.6 s raised-cosine 0->400->rest sequence that
also drags the slider; "Return to mirror" ramps to 0; "Step to onset" parks at 1.04 x B_c;
"Clear probe" dismisses the callout. Clicking the stage probes the nearest crest and
shows height, local flux density, local wavelength and distance from the wall in a
bordered callout; clicking below onset instead states that no crest exists and quotes the
onset. Hovering or focusing a nanoparticle in Plate I reveals its core/shell/carrier
label strip and an accent ring. Readouts, the B(t) trace, Plate II's dispersion curve and
the exhibition caption numbers all recompute from the same functions, so every number on
the page is derived from sigma, rho, the particle moment and the two sliders.
prefers-reduced-motion renders one settled frame (no rAF, fixed specular phase, controls
still work via an immediate re-render). The boot always paints one frame synchronously and
the animation loop keeps a 34 ms setTimeout alongside requestAnimationFrame, skipping a
timer tick whenever rAF is alive, so the cabinet still animates in hosts that never
service rAF (headless capture, some embedded webviews).
